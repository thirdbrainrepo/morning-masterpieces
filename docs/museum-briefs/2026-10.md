## Top tie-in opportunities, ranked

### 1. THE CAMERA LEARNS TO MAKE A BODY — buildable now, at full strength through Jan 18
- **Solid Pictures: Photosculpture and the Making of Modern Likeness** — Cantor,
  **Sep 17 2026 – Jan 18 2027**. François Willème's 1859 process (24 cameras in a
  circle, pantograph, cast) — the ancestor of 3D scanning.
  https://museum.stanford.edu/exhibitions/solid-pictures
- **Eternal Paris: Photographing the City of Light** — Legion of Honor,
  **Sep 5 2026 – Apr 4 2027**. ~50 photographs of Paris, 19th–20th c.
  https://www.famsf.org/exhibitions/eternal-paris-photographing-city-light

**Pitch — *"Solid Pictures: Paris Learns to Copy Itself"*:** promoted to #1
because both halves are open and overlapping and nothing in it has a rights
question. Second Empire Paris invented two ways to freeze a thing — Marville
photographing streets Haussmann was about to demolish, Willème photographing
citizens into bronze. Nadar, Carjat, Disdéri cartes-de-visite (Met, Getty, Paris
Musées) · Marville and Atget (Met CC0, NGA) · Gustave Le Gray (Getty, Met) ·
**Muybridge**, commissioned by Leland Stanford on the campus where *Solid
Pictures* hangs · Marey. The city stays a photography town all autumn
(Fraenkel's Robert Adams from Oct 29, *Feel the Beat* and Iturbide at SFMOMA,
*Ponte City* in January). It also opens a medium the gallery has never touched,
which is a reason to build it rather than a reason to wait.

### 2. EGYPTOMANIA — still the biggest audience, four months left
- **Treasures of the Pharaohs** — de Young, **Aug 1 2026 – Jan 31 2027**.
  ~130 objects spanning 3,000 years, including the US debut of finds from Luxor's
  "Golden City." https://www.famsf.org/exhibitions/treasures-pharaohs

**Pitch:** unchanged. David Roberts' *Egypt and Nubia* lithographs (NGA, Yale,
Getty) · *Description de l'Égypte* plates · Gérôme and Alma-Tadema (Met, AIC) ·
Frith and Du Camp photography (Met CC0) · Met / Cleveland / Brooklyn CC0 object
photography. Dropped to #2 only because the window is shrinking and #1 is the
better *new-medium* bet; if only one exhibition gets built this year, this is
still the safe one.

### 3. THE MASK — six months of runway, three venues
- **Klee Behind the Mask** — SFMOMA, **Aug 8 2026 – Apr 18 2027**.
  https://www.sfmoma.org/exhibition/klee-behind-the-mask/
- **One Hundred Plays of the Noh Theater by Tsukioka Kōgyō** — Asian, ongoing.
  https://exhibitions.asianart.org/exhibitions/one-hundred-plays-of-the-noh-theater-by-tsukioka-kogyo-2/
- **In Dialogue: African Arts** — Cantor, ongoing, free.
  https://museum.stanford.edu/exhibitions/dialogue-african-arts

**Pitch — *"Behind the Mask"*:** unchanged from September. Kōgyō (d. 1927; PD and
physically hanging at the Asian) · Ensor's 1880s–90s mask paintings and etchings ·
Callot's *Balli di Sfessania* (Met CC0) · Redon · Goya's *Caprichos* · Noh and
Kyōgen mask object photography (Met / Cleveland CC0). No rush; the best
spring-season build.

### 4. THE AMERICAN WATERCOLOR — new this month, a season early on purpose
- **Spectacular Freedom: Andrew Wyeth and the Modern American Watercolor** —
  de Young, **Feb 13 – Jul 18 2027** (secondary-sourced; tour confirmed by
  Cleveland, where it runs Sep 20 2026 – Jan 18 2027).
  https://www.clevelandart.org/exhibitions/spectacular-freedom-andrew-wyeth-and-modern-american-watercolor

**Pitch — *"Spectacular Freedom, Before Wyeth"*:** Wyeth (d. 2009) is firmly in
copyright and nothing of his goes near the gallery. But the exhibition's own
title argues that watercolor became a modern American medium, and the people who
made it one are deep in open access: **Winslow Homer** (d. 1910 — Bahamas,
Adirondacks, Prouts Neck; Met, AIC, NGA, Brooklyn, Cleveland all CC0-heavy) ·
**John Singer Sargent**'s watercolors (Met, Brooklyn) · Maurice Prendergast
(d. 1924) · Thomas Moran's Yellowstone field sketches · John La Farge · Charles
Demuth's pre-1931 flower and fruit sheets (published before the US-PD cutoff;
check each file's flag). Also a real wallpaper question worth testing early:
watercolor's white paper and soft edges may sit better on the dark matte than
oil does — or may fight it. Cleveland has the show now; a primer published by
February is the goal.

### 5. THE GOD WHO DREAMS THE WORLD — the Vishnu arrives Oct 23

the list.

## Wildcards — should not be missed

Three qualify. The first two are the strongest pair since the Kallat closed in
March, and they open and close within a month of each other — this is the season.

### 1. New Work: Raven Chacon — SFMOMA, Oct 24 2026 – Nov 7 2027
https://www.sfmoma.org/exhibition/new-work-raven-chacon/

*The Naming of Names* lays Bradbury's *Martian Chronicles* over 1950s–60s
travelers' footage of the Navajo Nation, setting Canyon de Chelly against Valles
Marineris and, in SFMOMA's phrase, collapsing time and space through Diné
worldviews; a drone soundtrack runs underneath. It opens in three weeks, and the
reason to go is the sound, which does not survive being read about.

The honest calibration: its center of gravity is political — who gets to name a
place — more than cosmic. But it uses the next planet over to make the argument,
and that is closer to the Kallat shape than anything else on the calendar.

### 2. Alexandre Estrela: RedSkyFalls — Wattis Institute, May 9 – Nov 21 2026
https://wattis.org/our-program/on-view/alexandre-estrela-i-redskyfalls-i

Animated creatures engraved in aluminum freeze and raise an alarm every time an
earthquake happens anywhere on Earth, in real time, with sister installations in
Lisbon, Lima, Mexico City and Los Angeles listening to the same planet. It is a
room that makes you a receiver for a signal that never stops.

New to this brief, and missed in September. Geological rather than cosmic, and
more quietly conceptual than overwhelming — but transmission at planetary scale,
in a city that sits on the fault, is exactly the territory. Six weeks left.

### 3. Vishnu's Cosmic Ocean — Asian Art Museum, Oct 23 2026 – Jan 25 2027
https://exhibitions.asianart.org/exhibitions/vishnus-cosmic-ocean/

A thousand-year-old bronze, nearly twenty feet long, of a god dreaming the
universe into being, pulled from a reservoir at Angkor — shown with praCh Ly's
film of a fisherman and a young monk living around that water now, and the sound
of it filling the gallery. A made thing that has carried a cosmology across a
millennium, and a film insisting the place kept living after the empire ended.

*Carried, not repeated:* **Sarah Sze: Forever is Composed of Nows** (SFMOMA atrium,
free, opens Nov 21) still holds its dates and remains the time-at-human-scale
alternative from September. **Susan Philipsz** on the 7th-floor terrace is a
worthy five-minute detour on any SFMOMA visit — a single voice singing to the
city — but longing, not deep time, so it stays a footnote.
