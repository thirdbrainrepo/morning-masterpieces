## Top tie-in opportunities, ranked

### 1. THE CAMERA LEARNS TO MAKE A BODY — build now; the Paris half may close Feb 28
- **Solid Pictures: Photosculpture and the Making of Modern Likeness** — Cantor,
  **Sep 17 2026 – Jan 18 2027**.
  https://museum.stanford.edu/exhibitions/solid-pictures
- **Eternal Paris: Photographing the City of Light** — Legion of Honor,
  **Sep 5 2026 – Feb 28 2027?** (Apr 4 in October; sources now conflict).

**Pitch — *"Solid Pictures: Paris Learns to Copy Itself"*:** unchanged. Marville
and Atget (Met CC0, NGA) · Nadar, Carjat, Disdéri cartes-de-visite (Met, Getty,
Paris Musées) · Le Gray (Getty, Met) · Muybridge · Marey. Still the cleanest
rights picture on the list and the first new medium. *Solid Pictures* closes
Jan 18 — a primer that isn't live by mid-December mostly misses it.

### 2. EGYPTOMANIA — three months left; build only if it can ship in November
- **Treasures of the Pharaohs** — de Young, **Aug 1 2026 – Jan 31 2027** (confirmed
  via Kimbell). https://www.famsf.org/exhibitions/treasures-pharaohs

**Pitch:** unchanged — David Roberts lithographs, *Description de l'Égypte*
plates, Gérôme, Frith and Du Camp, Met/Cleveland/Brooklyn CC0 objects. The
honest read: the window is closing, and #3 below has two years of runway. Build
this only as a short exhibition, or let it go.

### 3. THE BAY AREA AS A PLACE IN MOTION — new, and the long game
- **California Current: Art and Histories from the Bay Area** — Cantor,
  **Oct 29 2026 – Oct 23 2028**, Freidenrich Gallery 221; part of the Further
  Triennial (*Around Here*, Mar 10 – Jun 10 2027).
  https://museum.stanford.edu/exhibitions/california-current-art-and-histories-bay-area

**Pitch — *"Before the Current: California Seen for the First Time"*:** the show
is 20th–21st c. and in copyright; the century that taught the world what
California looked like is not. **Carleton Watkins** (Yosemite and the
Bay mammoth plates; Met CC0, Getty, NGA) · **Eadweard Muybridge**'s 1878
San Francisco panorama (Getty, Library of Congress) · **Albert Bierstadt**,
**Thomas Hill**, **William Keith** (Met, NGA, Smithsonian, Yale) · Arthur and
Lucia Mathews (pre-1931 works; check each flag) · Japanese and Chinese
export views of 19th-c. San Francisco. It makes a natural sequel to #1 (Muybridge
again, the same campus), and it is the first exhibition that could be timed to a
regional festival rather than one museum. Target: live for Triennial week in March.

### 4. THE MASK — five months of runway, three venues
- **Klee Behind the Mask** — SFMOMA, **Aug 8 2026 – Apr 18 2027**.
- **One Hundred Plays of the Noh Theater by Tsukioka Kōgyō** — Asian, ongoing.
- **In Dialogue: African Arts** — Cantor, ongoing, free.

**Pitch — *"Behind the Mask"*:** unchanged. Kōgyō (PD and physically on the wall) ·
Ensor · Callot's *Balli di Sfessania* · Redon · Goya's *Caprichos* · Noh and Kyōgen
masks (Met/Cleveland CC0). Still the best spring-season build.

### 5. THE AMERICAN WATERCOLOR — dates now confirmed

## Wildcards — should not be missed

Three qualify, ordered by urgency. One leaves in three weeks.

### 1. Alexandre Estrela: RedSkyFalls — Wattis Institute, May 9 – Nov 21 2026
https://wattis.org/our-program/on-view/alexandre-estrela-i-redskyfalls-i

Projected creatures freeze and an alarm sounds every time the Earth quakes
anywhere, in real time, with sister sites in other seismic cities listening to
the same planet. It is a room that turns you into a receiver for a signal
that never stops, in a city that sits on the fault.

Geological rather than cosmic, quiet rather than overwhelming; corrected this
month (video projection, not engraved aluminum). **Last call — three weeks.**

### 2. Sarah Sze: Forever is Composed of Nows — SFMOMA atrium, opens Nov 21 2026, free
https://www.sfmoma.org/exhibition/sarah-sze/

Three monumental paintings, video projections and pulsing sound spread across
the atrium and several floors, on "the passage of time" and images that don't
last — the title is Dickinson's, and Sze's framing is time as a continuum of
present moments adding up to forever. Of everything new this month it states
the target themes most directly, as an environment rather than objects on a wall.

Promoted from footnote. Honest caveats: no closing date, so not urgent; nobody
has seen it yet; and Sze's image swarms can dazzle more than they move. Go once
the opening crowds and atrium construction clear.

### 3. Vishnu's Cosmic Ocean — Asian Art Museum, Oct 23 2026 – Jan 25 2027
https://exhibitions.asianart.org/exhibitions/vishnus-cosmic-ocean/

The West Mebon Vishnu, nearly twenty feet of thousand-year-old bronze, a god
dreaming the universe into being, shown at full scale with praCh Ly's film and
the sound of the reservoir it came out of. The National Museum of Cambodia's
director calls this the last chance to see it outside Cambodia. A made thing
that carried a cosmology across a millennium, about to go home for good.

*Carried, not repeated:* **Raven Chacon, *The Naming of Names*** (SFMOMA, through
Nov 7 2027) is open now and still the right call; a year to run, so not urgent.
**Near-miss:** **Scott Snibbe, *Hidden Geometries*** at Headlands (Sep 20 –
Nov 16; open Thu–Sun 12–5, but closed Nov 7 and 14, so effectively Nov 5, 6, 8,
12, 13, 15) — Tibetan thangka grids in mineral pigment with live generative
light and guided meditations; the only thing this month aimed squarely at
consciousness, but a residency project space that can't be vouched for from a
web page. **Next season:** BAMPFA's *MATRIX: 50 Years* (Jan 23) — one program
handed across five decades, the 1978 founding projects restaged — is the
candidate to watch for continuity and transmission.
