## Top tie-in opportunities, ranked

### 1. EGYPTOMANIA — still the headline, five months of runway left
- **Treasures of the Pharaohs** — de Young, **Aug 1 2026 – Jan 31 2027**.
  130 objects from the Egyptian Museum in Cairo and the Luxor Museum spanning
  3,000 years, many shown in North America for the first time.
  https://www.famsf.org/exhibitions/treasures-pharaohs

**Pitch — *"Egyptomania"*:** unchanged from August, and unchanged is the point —
it is the only mass-audience show on the calendar with a deep, entirely
unencumbered PD bench and no rights cliff anywhere in it. David Roberts'
*Egypt and Nubia* lithographs (NGA, Yale, Getty) · the *Description de l'Égypte*
plates, which are the literal origin of the craze · Gérôme (d. 1904) and
Alma-Tadema (d. 1912) at the Met and AIC · Francis Frith and Maxime Du Camp's
monument photography, Met CC0 · Met / Cleveland / Brooklyn CC0 *museum
photography of Egyptian objects*, which lets a lesson show the visitor the kind
of thing they will actually stand in front of. Still my recommendation for
exhibition #2 after the permanent collection.

### 2. THE CAMERA LEARNS TO MAKE A BODY — no longer prospective; both halves are open
- **Solid Pictures: Photosculpture and the Making of Modern Likeness** —
  Cantor, **Sep 17 2026 – Jan 18 2027**. The first exhibition ever devoted to
  François Willème's 1859 process: 24 cameras in a circle, silhouettes traced
  by pantograph, assembled and cast. A commercial failure, and the ancestor of
  3D scanning and additive manufacturing. Curated by Patrick R. Crowley.
  https://museum.stanford.edu/exhibitions/solid-pictures
- **Eternal Paris: Photographing the City of Light** — Legion of Honor,
  **Sep 5 2026 – Apr 4 2027** (extended five weeks since August).
  https://www.famsf.org/exhibitions/eternal-paris-photographing-city-light

**Pitch — *"Solid Pictures: Paris Learns to Copy Itself"*:** Second Empire Paris
invented two ways to freeze a thing — Marville photographing streets Haussmann
was about to demolish, Willème photographing citizens into bronze — and both
were arguments about what survives. Entirely PD, and it opens a medium the
gallery has never touched: Nadar, Carjat, Disdéri's cartes-de-visite (Met,
Getty, Paris Musées) · Marville and Atget (Met CC0, NGA) · Gustave Le Gray
(Getty, Met) · **Eadweard Muybridge** (d. 1904), whose *Animal Locomotion* was
commissioned by Leland Stanford, on the campus where *Solid Pictures* hangs ·
Marey's chronophotography.

What changed: in August this was a bet on two shows that had not opened. Both
are open or open in two weeks, they overlap **Sep 17 – Jan 18** at full
strength, and the city has independently turned into a photography town this
autumn (Fraenkel's Friedlander and Robert Adams shows, SFMOMA's *Feel the Beat*
running to Feb 28 2027). This is the one that is buildable *now*.

### 3. THE MASK — best new idea this month, and a free second venue
Three concurrent shows in three institutions, none of which mention each other:
- **Klee Behind the Mask** — SFMOMA, **Aug 8 2026 – Apr 18 2027**. How the mask
  became the hinge between ancient ritual, modern psychology, and popular
  entertainment; Klee's post-WWI turn to abstraction as a healing process, and
  the circulation of masks through Dada and Surrealism.
  https://www.sfmoma.org/exhibition/klee-behind-the-mask/
- **One Hundred Plays of the Noh Theater by Tsukioka Kōgyō** — Asian, ongoing.
  https://exhibitions.asianart.org/exhibitions/one-hundred-plays-of-the-noh-theater-by-tsukioka-kogyo-2/
- **In Dialogue: African Arts** — Cantor, ongoing, free.
  https://museum.stanford.edu/exhibitions/dialogue-african-arts

**Pitch — *"Behind the Mask"*:** the mask is the oldest technology for being
someone else, and European modernism did not invent its interest in it — it
arrived late and took credit. The PD bench is unusually clean because the
keystone figures all died early enough:
- **Tsukioka Kōgyō** (d. 1927) — his Noh series is PD *and* physically hanging
  at the Asian right now, so a lesson can send the reader to the actual object
- **James Ensor**'s mask paintings and etchings of the 1880s–90s (published
  well before 1930, US-PD) — the direct precursor Klee's show implies but
  cannot show much of
- Jacques Callot, *Balli di Sfessania* (1622) commedia dell'arte prints — Met CC0
- Odilon Redon (d. 1916) — Met, AIC, Rijksmuseum
- Japanese Noh and Kyōgen mask **museum photography**, Met / Cleveland CC0
- Ensor's contemporaries in Belgian symbolism, plus Goya's *Caprichos*, where
  the mask is already doing psychological work a century early

Eight months of runway, three venues, two of them free, and the argument writes
itself. My pick for the most interesting *new* thing on the calendar.

### 4. THE GOD WHO DREAMS THE WORLD — now a trio, and the third leg changes it
- **Art from Contemporary Cambodia** — Asian, **Aug 27 2026 – Jan 25 2027**.
  Sopheap Pich and Leang Seckon, both born in the 1970s and both survivors of
  the Khmer Rouge; Pich's abstract sculpture in rattan and found material,
  Seckon's collage in photographs, paint, and recycled textile.
  https://exhibitions.asianart.org/exhibitions/art-from-contemporary-cambodia/
- **Garuda in Bali** — Asian, **Sep 18 2026 – Jan 4 2027**.
  https://exhibitions.asianart.org/exhibitions/garuda-in-bali/
- **Vishnu's Cosmic Ocean** — Asian, **Oct 23 2026 – Jan 25 2027**. The West
  Mebon Reclining Vishnu, ~20 feet of Angkor bronze.
  https://exhibitions.asianart.org/exhibitions/vishnus-cosmic-ocean/

**Pitch — *"The God Who Dreams the World"*:** August had this as a pair. The
Cambodia show, which opened Aug 27, makes it a trio and quietly makes it better:
the Angkor bronze and the Balinese Garuda are the cosmology, and Pich and Seckon
are what happened to the people who inherited it. All three run concurrently
**Oct 23 – Jan 4**. PD support from Met and Cleveland CC0 — Khmer and Cham
bronze, Indian Vishnu stelae, Balinese painting, wayang, Company School and
Kangra Vishnu paintings. Still the best-looking wallpaper material on this list.

### Watch, don't build
- **Azzedine Alaïa: Sculpting Fashion** — Legion, Nov 21 2026 – Apr 11 2027.
  The month's biggest new announcement and an honest miss for us. The PD angle
  that exists — 18th/19th-century fashion plates, Met Costume Institute CC0
  object photography, Vigée Le Brun's dressmaking eye — is a real thing, but it
  is a costume-history lesson wearing a painting lesson's clothes, and the
  gallery has no vocabulary for it yet. Revisit if SFMOMA's **Dior to McQueen**
  (Apr 17 2027) makes fashion a two-museum season worth building a vocabulary
  for; that would be the moment, not this one.
- **Some Particular Heaven: Ideas of Utopia** — BAMPFA, thru Jul 11 2027.
  Eleven months, thematic rather than period-bound. Now pairs with BAMPFA's
  Teaching Gallery show on colonization, nuclear legacies, and climate
  (Aug 26 2026 – Jan 24 2027). Best use is still a **permanent-collection-drawn
  theme exhibition** — no new acquisitions needed, which makes it the cheapest
  exhibition on this list to actually ship.
- **Feel the Beat: Dance in Photographs** — SFMOMA, thru Feb 28 2027. Still best
  as a cross-reference from the existing Degas lesson.
- **Miró: Proof Against All** — Legion, thru Jul 25 2027. Miró (d. 1983) and
  Éluard (d. 1952) are firmly in copyright and the honest PD precursors are a
  stretch. Unchanged from August.
- **Go Your Own Way: Northern California Craft, 1945–80** — SFMOMA, Feb 13 –
  Nov 14 2027. Nine months, 100 makers, 200 objects. Too far out to plan, but
  it is the kind of long local show that rewards a primer, and Ruth Asawa is
  currently on view in three places at once (Cantor's *Faces of Ruth Asawa*,
  Minnesota Street Project's *Endless Variations* thru Oct 3, SFMOMA's
  collection). Flagging it a season early on purpose.

## Wildcards — should not be missed

Three qualify this month, and one of them is the strongest candidate since the
Kallat piece closed in March.

### 1. New Work: Raven Chacon — SFMOMA, Oct 24 2026 – Nov 7 2027
https://www.sfmoma.org/exhibition/new-work-raven-chacon/

Chacon, the first Native American composer to win the Pulitzer in music, has
built a five-channel installation called *The Naming of Names* that lays Ray
Bradbury's Martian fiction over archival footage from the 1950s and '60s, with
field recordings and electronics underneath, and draws topographical comparisons
between Tséyi' — Canyon de Chelly — and Valles Marineris on Mars. It is about
what happens when people arrive somewhere that already has names, and it uses
the planet next door to say it about this one.

That is the Kallat shape: a work about transmission and what our made things
carry forward, built by someone who takes the cosmos literally rather than
decoratively. It opens during the US Semiquincentennial, which is not an
accident, and it runs thirteen months, so there is no rush — but the reason to
go is the sound, and sound does not survive being read about.

### 2. Vishnu's Cosmic Ocean — Asian Art Museum, Oct 23 2026 – Jan 25 2027
https://exhibitions.asianart.org/exhibitions/vishnus-cosmic-ocean/

Carried from August, now with firm dates inside the horizon. A thousand-year-old
bronze, nearly twenty feet long, of a god lying on a serpent dreaming the
universe into being — pulled from a reservoir at Angkor, shown with a praCh Ly
film about the people living around that water now and the sound of it filling
the gallery.

An object built to hold a cosmology, still holding it a millennium after
everyone who believed it that way is gone, and a film insisting the place did
not stop existing when the empire did. With *Art from Contemporary Cambodia*
open down the hall, the museum has accidentally built the full argument.

### 3. Sarah Sze: Forever is Composed of Nows — SFMOMA, opens Nov 21 2026, free
https://www.sfmoma.org/exhibition/sarah-sze/

Three monumental paintings, video projection, and sound spilling up through
multiple floors of the Haas Atrium, built around the ephemerality of images and
the passage of time; the title is Dickinson's, and the argument is that
permanence is only ever a pile of present moments. It is free, it is in the
atrium you walk through anyway, and it is ongoing rather than ticketed.

The honest caveat: Sze's work is about time at human scale — attention,
accumulation, the half-life of an image — not deep time or the cosmos. It clears
the "profound first-person experience" bar comfortably and lands one ring in
from the themes that make the Kallat piece the standard. Worth an hour, and
worth going twice, because it changes with the light.

*Note for the atrium: Samia Halaby's* Kinetic Paintings *— four 1987–88 works,
made on an Amiga, running on the big screen in the same space since Jan 24 —
almost certainly comes down for Sze. If a pioneer of computer-generated painting
shown at monumental scale is of interest, that is a before-November errand.*
