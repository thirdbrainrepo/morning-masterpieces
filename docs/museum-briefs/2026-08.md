## Top tie-in opportunities, ranked

### 1. EGYPTOMANIA — the headline, and the runway is now open
- **Treasures of the Pharaohs** — de Young, **Aug 1 2026 – Jan 31 2027**.
  130 artifacts from the Egyptian Museum in Cairo and the Luxor Museum,
  3,000 years; many in North America for the first time. Curated by Tarek
  El Awady; FAMSF presentation by Renée Dreyfus.
  https://www.famsf.org/exhibitions/treasures-pharaohs

**Pitch — *"Egyptomania"*:** how ancient Egypt seized the Western imagination,
and what that says about looking at someone else's dead. Six months of overlap,
a mass-audience show, and the deepest PD bench of anything on this list:
- David Roberts, *Egypt and Nubia* lithographs (1846–49) — NGA, Yale, Getty
- The *Description de l'Égypte* plates from Napoleon's savants — public domain
  outright, gorgeous, and the actual origin of the craze
- Jean-Léon Gérôme (d. 1904) and Lawrence Alma-Tadema (d. 1912) — Met, AIC
- Early photography of the monuments: Francis Frith, Maxime Du Camp — Met CC0
- Met / Cleveland / Brooklyn CC0 **museum photography of Egyptian objects**
  (museum-own photos of 3D antiquities; cleanly licensed, and lets us show
  the kind of thing the visitor will actually stand in front of)

This should be exhibition #2 after the permanent collection. Relaxed build
schedule, no rights cliff anywhere in the list.

### 2. THE CAMERA LEARNS TO MAKE A BODY — best new idea this month
Two shows, two museums, one decade, and they have not noticed each other:
- **Solid Pictures: Photosculpture and the Making of Modern Likeness** —
  Cantor, **Sep 17 2026 – Jan 18 2027**. The first exhibition ever on
  photosculpture: François Willème's 1859 process — 24 cameras in a circle,
  silhouettes traced by pantograph, assembled and cast. Commercially a
  failure; ancestrally, 3D scanning.
  https://museum.stanford.edu/exhibitions/solid-pictures
- **Eternal Paris: Photographing the City of Light** — Legion of Honor,
  **Sep 5 2026 – Feb 28 2027**. ~50 photographs, 19th/early-20th c.
  https://www.famsf.org/exhibitions/eternal-paris-photographing-city-light

**Pitch — *"Solid Pictures: Paris Learns to Copy Itself"*:** Second Empire
Paris invented two ways to freeze a thing — Marville photographing streets
Haussmann was about to demolish, Willème photographing citizens into bronze —
and both were arguments about what survives. Entirely PD, and it opens a medium
we've never touched:
- Nadar, Étienne Carjat, Disdéri's cartes-de-visite — Met, Getty, Paris Musées
- Charles Marville's Haussmann-era Paris and Eugène Atget — Met CC0, NGA
- Gustave Le Gray — Getty, Met
- **Eadweard Muybridge** (d. 1904) *Animal Locomotion* — and the Stanford hook
  writes itself: Muybridge's motion studies were commissioned by Leland
  Stanford, on the campus where *Solid Pictures* hangs. Met, NGA, Smithsonian.
- Étienne-Jules Marey's chronophotography

Two venues, four months of overlap, a local origin story, and a subject
(technology reaching for likeness) that our audience lives inside. My pick for
the most interesting thing on the calendar.

### 3. A GALLERY OF THEIR OWN — long runway, obvious PD counterweight
- **A Gallery of Their Own: Women Artists from the Carr and Goldblum Gift** —
  de Young, **Aug 15 2026 – Feb 28 2027**. Women artists active since the
  1960s: Bourgeois, Dater, DeFeo, Krasner, Kusama.
  https://www.famsf.org/exhibitions/gallery-women-artists-carr-goldblum

**Pitch — *"The Gallery They Didn't Have"*:** the PD half of the same argument,
running centuries earlier. Artemisia Gentileschi, Judith Leyster, Élisabeth
Vigée Le Brun, Angelica Kauffman, Rosa Bonheur, Berthe Morisot (d. 1895), Mary
Cassatt (d. 1926), Julia Margaret Cameron. Met / AIC / NGA / Rijksmuseum are
deep in all of them, and Cassatt and Morisot already have PC neighbors. Six and
a half months of overlap.

### 4. VISHNU AND HIS MOUNT — now a pair, not a footnote
- **Garuda in Bali** — Asian, **Sep 18 2026 – Jan 4 2027**. Balinese wooden
  sculpture and painting, late 19th/early 20th c.
  https://exhibitions.asianart.org/exhibitions/garuda-in-bali/
- **Vishnu's Cosmic Ocean** — Asian, **Oct 23 2026 – Jan 25 2027**. The West
  Mebon Reclining Vishnu, ~20 feet of Angkor bronze.
  https://exhibitions.asianart.org/exhibitions/vishnus-cosmic-ocean/

**Pitch — *"The God Who Dreams the World"*:** Garuda is Vishnu's vehicle, so
these two shows are one story told from opposite ends, and the Asian is running
them concurrently for three months. PD support from Met and Cleveland CC0 —
Khmer and Cham bronze, Indian Vishnu stelae, Indonesian wayang and Balinese
painting, plus Company School and Kangra Vishnu paintings. Also the single
best-looking wallpaper material on this list. See also the wildcard section.

## Wildcards — should not be missed

### 1. Vishnu's Cosmic Ocean — Asian Art Museum, Oct 23 2026 – Jan 25 2027
https://exhibitions.asianart.org/exhibitions/vishnus-cosmic-ocean/

A thousand-year-old bronze, nearly twenty feet long, of a god lying on a
serpent dreaming the universe into existence — pulled from a reservoir at
Angkor, shown with a praCh Ly film of the people living around that water now
and the sound of it filling the gallery. This is the calibration example's
shape almost exactly: an object built to hold a cosmology, still holding it a
millennium after everyone who believed it that way is gone, and a film insisting
the place did not stop existing when the empire did.

### 2. Life in Space — Exploratorium, thru Sep 13 2026 ⚠️ last call
https://www.exploratorium.edu/visit/calendar/space

Fifty-plus exhibits on how life — ours or anyone's — might breathe, eat, and
travel beyond Earth, including *Sound on Other Worlds* and spacesuit replicas
lent by Adam Savage. It is the nearest living relative to the Kallat Golden
Record piece: the same question about what we send outward and what it would
mean to be received, handled as physics rather than as elegy — which is either
the honest version or the smaller one, and worth going to find out before it
closes.

### 3. Jacob Hashimoto: Giant Arc — SFMOMA, opens Aug 22 2026, free
https://www.sfmoma.org/exhibition/jacob-hashimoto-giant-arc/

More than 75,000 hand-built bamboo-and-paper kites filling the ground-floor
gallery wall to wall and floor to ceiling, the artist's largest work to date,
and free to walk into off Howard Street. Honest caveat: this is an encounter
with scale and patience — 75,000 objects each made by hand — rather than with
deep time or transmission, so it clears the "profound first-person experience"
bar without quite landing on the themes that make the Kallat piece the standard.
